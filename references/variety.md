# Variety pools

Two of the three axes a scenario is built from. The third, mode, lives in SKILL.md. Pick from these with the rules in "Picking a scenario — variety rules." All settings are generic product types; invent the company every time.

None of these settings repeat the 30 in `scenarios.md`, so the pool and the seed bank together cover 90 settings.

## Problem shapes — 23

Each shape is the thing underneath. The named pattern in the close usually comes straight from it.

1. **A system took over a human judgment.** The target metric improved; the cost landed where the metric can't see it.
2. **Two users, one interface, opposite wants.** Whose clock or whose calendar does it run on?
3. **The workaround is the spec.** People built something on the side, and it's better than the product.
4. **Fixing the bottleneck moved it.** Speeding up one step only moved the queue downstream.
5. **The thing that works isn't sanctioned.** The real process lives off the books.
6. **Right diagnosis, wrong owner.** You can see what's broken and can't touch it.
7. **It only works at the good sites.** Same product, very different results, and nobody's asked why.
8. **The population changed, not the product.** The metric improved because different people are being counted.
9. **Success created a load nobody staffed.** Adoption worked; the people behind it are drowning.
10. **The approval is in the wrong place.** A sign-off happens at the moment it can do the least good.
11. **A threshold nobody can explain.** A number in the flow is a design decision nobody owns.
12. **The metric counts the claim, not the outcome.** Someone tapped "done"; the thing isn't done.
13. **A default is doing the deciding.** Preselected options count as consent only if people saw them.
14. **The friction was doing a job.** A removed step was quietly catching problems.
15. **An estimate gets read as a promise.** A projection shown to users becomes a commitment.
16. **The average hides two groups.** One population is thriving and another is stuck; the mean says "fine."
17. **One switch can't hold the preference.** A single toggle captures something that varies by item, time or person.
18. **The gap between two systems belongs to nobody.** Each system works; the user falls through the seam.
19. **The tool and the decider use different rules.** The product predicts one outcome and a person decides another.
20. **A proxy became the goal.** People optimize the measurable stand-in and the real thing drifts.
21. **The win and the loss hit different people.** One group pays for another group's improvement.
22. **A rule built for the median strands the edge.** A sensible limit works for most and leaves a few with no option at all.
23. **The handoff drops the context.** Information gathered at step one never reaches the person at step three.

## Modes — reminder

Discovery, interpretation, commitment. Commitment and interpretation are the ones that get under-served; when in doubt, pick one of them.
